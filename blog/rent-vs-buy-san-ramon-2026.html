Rent vs Buy in San Ramon: The 2026 Math

Manish Anand, REALTOR® · CA DRE #02247006
Updated October 7, 2026 8 min read

Short answer: In San Ramon, renting is far cheaper per month than buying, and the gap is wide. Zillow data for August 2026 shows a typical home value of about $1,451,174 and a typical asking rent of about $3,246 per month. With 20% down at 7.28%, the same home costs roughly $10,853 per month to own under the assumptions below. Buying can still make sense if you plan to stay many years and value ownership, but the monthly math favors renting.

This guide shows the arithmetic so you can check it, and says plainly what the comparison leaves out. It is general information, not financial or tax advice.

Quick take: Price-to-rent ratio about 37.3. Owning costs about $7,607 more per month than renting at typical values. Home values are down about 3.6% year over year while typical rent is up about 4.1%.

What do typical home values and rents look like in San Ramon right now?

Typical home value (Zillow Home Value Index, single-family and condo, middle price tier) for San Ramon was $1,451,174 in August 2026. Typical asking rent (Zillow Observed Rent Index) was $3,246 per month in August 2026. These are index values for a typical home, not a median sale price or a quote for any listing.

One caution: the rent index blends single-family homes, condos and apartments, while the value index covers single-family homes and condos. A three-bedroom house in San Ramon will often rent for more than the blended figure, which narrows the gap somewhat. For sale-price detail by neighborhood, see San Ramon home prices in 2026 and the San Ramon city guide.

What is the price-to-rent ratio in San Ramon?

The price-to-rent ratio divides the home value by one year of rent.

  • Annual rent: $3,246 x 12 = $38,953
  • Price-to-rent ratio: $1,451,174 / $38,953 = about 37.3
  • Gross rent yield: $38,953 / $1,451,174 = about 2.7%

A common rule of thumb treats ratios above roughly 20 as favoring renting on a pure cash basis. At 37, San Ramon sits well above that line, as many high-priced Bay Area suburbs do. The ratio is a screen, not a verdict.

What is the property tax rate in San Ramon?

California's base property tax is about 1% of assessed value under Proposition 13, plus voter-approved local levies such as school and bond measures. The total effective rate varies by parcel, so the 1.2% above is an illustration, not a San Ramon rate. Homes in newer areas such as Dougherty Valley may also carry Mello-Roos (community facilities district) special taxes. See how Mello-Roos works for the mechanics, and check the tax bill or title report for any specific home.

What does the monthly comparison leave out?

  • Opportunity cost of the down payment. The $290,235 down payment could be invested instead. If it earned an assumed 4% per year, that is about $11,609 per year, or about $967 per month. Returns are not guaranteed.
  • Transaction costs. Buyer closing costs are commonly 2% to 3% of the price, or about $29,023 to $43,535 on this example. Selling a home has its own costs, including agent compensation, transfer taxes and prep. These make short holding periods expensive.
  • Appreciation is uncertain. Zillow's index shows San Ramon values lower than a year ago. Equity from appreciation is a possible gain, not a given.
  • Tax effects. Mortgage interest and property tax may be deductible if you itemize, subject to limits. See IRS Publication 936 and ask a tax professional.
  • Rent can rise. Typical rent is up about 4.1% over a year, while a fixed-rate principal and interest payment does not change.
  • Non-financial value. Stability, control over the home and no landlord decisions matter to many households.

How long do you need to stay for buying to make sense?

This article does not give a break-even number, because it hinges on appreciation, rent growth, your investment returns and tax situation, none of which are known in advance. Qualitatively: when owning costs thousands more per month than renting, buying needs a long holding period to recover transaction costs and the monthly gap. A short horizon of a few years rarely favors buying at these prices and rates. Put your own assumptions into the buy vs rent calculator to see how the answer moves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in San Ramon in 2026?

Renting is cheaper month to month by a wide margin. Using Zillow data for August 2026, the typical San Ramon home value is about $1,451,174 and the typical asking rent is about $3,246 per month. With 20% down at a 7.28% rate (Freddie Mac, October 1, 2026) and illustrative tax, insurance and maintenance assumptions, owning costs roughly $10,853 per month. Buying is a long-horizon wealth decision, not a monthly savings play.

What is the price-to-rent ratio in San Ramon?

About 37. The typical home value of $1,451,174 divided by a year of typical rent ($3,246 x 12 = $38,953) is roughly 37.3. Ratios well above 20 generally favor renting on a pure monthly cash basis, though the ratio ignores appreciation, taxes and how long you stay.

How much is rent in San Ramon?

The Zillow Observed Rent Index put typical asking rent in San Ramon at about $3,246 per month in August 2026, up about 4.1% from about $3,119 in August 2025. This index blends single-family homes, condos and apartments, so a comparable single-family house will usually rent for more.

How long do I need to stay for buying to beat renting in San Ramon?

There is no single number. With a gap of several thousand dollars per month between owning and renting at current rates, buying generally needs a long holding period, steady appreciation or meaningful rent growth to catch up. Run your own numbers in the buy vs rent calculator before deciding.

Are San Ramon home values going up or down?

Down over the past year. Zillow's typical home value for San Ramon was about $1,451,174 in August 2026 versus about $1,505,490 in August 2025, a decline of about 3.6%. Past trends do not predict future prices.

What is the property tax rate in San Ramon?

The base California rate is about 1% of assessed value under Proposition 13, plus voter-approved local levies, so the total varies by parcel. Newer areas such as Dougherty Valley can also carry Mello-Roos (community facilities district) special taxes. Check the specific parcel's tax bill; this article uses 1.2% only as a clearly labeled illustration.
