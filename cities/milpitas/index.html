🚇 CITY GUIDE

Living in Milpitas, California

Silicon Valley's front door — commuter-friendly prices at the Bay Area's crossroads

Median SFH: $1.2M–$1.5M

Range reflects recent Zillow ZHVI data · live figure loads when available · see the full market table

Milpitas sits at the Bay Area's crossroads — where I-880 meets I-680 at the foot of the East Bay hills — with its own BART station, a top-10% California school district, and single-family homes from around $1.2M. For buyers priced out of San Jose and Cupertino, it's the nearest pressure valve.

The city blends established central neighborhoods with transit-oriented development around the Milpitas Transit Center, drawing tech commuters who want rail access without Peninsula prices.

BART access

Milpitas station (opened 2020) links the Berryessa Extension to the Bay Area rail network.

Top schools

Milpitas Unified ranks in California's top 10% with a ~94% graduation rate.

Relative value

Single-family homes $1.2M–$1.5M — notably cheaper than Cupertino or Palo Alto.

Job proximity

15–20 min to downtown San Jose and Fremont; 25–35 min to Palo Alto.

EDUCATION

Schools in Milpitas

Milpitas Unified School District Milpitas USD serves almost the entire city and ranks in the top 10% of California districts with roughly a 94% graduation rate — so most buyers get Milpitas USD schools without attendance-boundary surprises.

  • Milpitas High School
  • Thomas Russell Middle School
  • John Sinnott Elementary School
  • Marshall Pomeroy Elementary School
GETTING AROUND

Commute & Connectivity

Milpitas has genuine multi-modal transit: BART, VTA light rail, and three freeways.

  • Milpitas BART station (Berryessa Extension, opened 2020) on the Green and Orange lines
  • VTA Orange Line light rail to Sunnyvale and Mountain View via the Transit Center
  • I-880, I-680, and CA-237 all serve the city
  • Downtown San Jose ~15–20 min; Fremont ~15 min; Santa Clara corridor ~20 min
NEIGHBORHOODS

Where to Look in Milpitas

Midtown

Central Milpitas along Main Street — older homes mixed with newer mixed-use infill under the Gateway-Main Street plan.

Calaveras Ridge

Hillside east side around Calaveras Road — larger lots and rural-leaning properties near Ed Levin County Park.

Great Mall / Transit Area

Transit-oriented development around BART and the Great Mall — condos and townhomes popular with commuters.

INVESTOR LENS

Milpitas for Investors

Cash flow is tight on long-term holds at a $1.2–$1.5M entry, so most investors are playing appreciation and 1031-exchange leverage rather than immediate yield. Tenant demand is durable: Milpitas sits between the Great Mall corridor employers and north San Jose tech campuses, with BART/VTA access drawing deep tech-renter demand — plus strong resale liquidity from first-time-buyer families priced out of San Jose and Cupertino.
